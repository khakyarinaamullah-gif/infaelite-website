When a redesign is worth it.

A redesign is a big job. These are the signs that it will pay for itself, rather than a few targeted fixes.

  • {{ic:smartphone}}

    Mobile feels like an afterthought

    Most shoppers browse on a phone. When menus, filters and product pages feel cramped there, the layout needs rethinking, not shrinking.

  • {{ic:gauge}}

    Pages are slow to load

    Years of apps and patches add scripts to every page. When key templates fail Core Web Vitals, a cleaner theme often fixes more than tuning the old one.

  • {{ic:layers}}

    The theme is patched together

    Vintage themes and heavy customizations make every change risky. Moving to an Online Store 2.0 theme brings sections on every page and safer updates.

  • {{ic:palette}}

    The brand has moved on

    New packaging, photography or positioning that the store does not reflect yet, so the first impression undersells the product.

  • {{ic:trending-up}}

    Visitors arrive but do not buy

    When shoppers reach product pages and leave, the fix is usually in the journey: product information, trust signals, the cart and the steps to checkout.

  • {{ic:search}}

    Search visibility has stalled

    Thin collection pages, duplicate content and missing structured data hold rankings back. A redesign is the right moment to fix the structure.

If only one or two of these apply, a focused Shopify optimization project may be enough. The audit will say which.

How a Shopify redesign runs.

The current store keeps trading until launch day. Each stage ends with something you can review.

  1. 01

    Audit

    We review analytics, the current theme, apps, speed and search data to find what is holding the store back.

    You get an audit with priorities.

  2. 02

    UX & conversion plan

    Journeys, page structure and content are planned around the findings, with a map of every URL that will move.

    You get a page plan and a URL map.

  3. 03

    Design

    New page designs for mobile and desktop, built on your brand and approved before development starts.

    You get approved designs.

  4. 04

    Build, unpublished

    The new theme is built in your store but hidden from shoppers, using your real products and content.

    You get a preview link to review.

  5. 05

    Test & compare

    Devices, browsers, cart, checkout, apps, speed and SEO are checked against the current store.

    You get a QA report with before and after speed results.

  6. 06

    Launch & measure

    We publish at a quiet time, add the redirects, watch orders and search coverage, and fix anything that slipped.

    You get a live redesign and a post-launch check.

How rankings and sales are protected.

A redesign should not cost the traffic the store has already earned. These steps are part of every launch.

  • {{ic:check}}The live store keeps trading on its current theme until launch day.
  • {{ic:check}}Product, collection and page URLs stay the same wherever possible.
  • {{ic:check}}Any URL that has to change gets a 301 redirect in Shopify’s URL redirects.
  • {{ic:check}}Page titles, meta descriptions and image alt text are carried over or improved, never dropped.
  • {{ic:check}}Structured data for products and breadcrumbs is checked on the new theme.
  • {{ic:check}}Analytics, pixels and tracking are tested before the new theme goes live.
  • {{ic:check}}Speed is measured on the same templates before and after, so the result is a number, not an opinion.
